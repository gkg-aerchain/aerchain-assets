02

The misread, and the recovery

Roughly the first half of the session was spent discovering — and then honestly correcting — a mismatch between what IndiaMart's referral implied and what we actually do.

What they wanted

An aggregator (Moglix / Amazon Business / L&T SuFin / IndiaMart / Flipkart) to host and maintain vendor catalogs externally, integrated into Ariba Guided Buying as an L2 punch-out. User picks items on the vendor portal, cart returns to Ariba, PR/PO completes there. Goal: automate tail spend and capture market-aggregated pricing.

What we had to clarify

We have hosted catalogs, vendor-hosted (L2) catalogs and true punch-outs of our own — but we are a technology platform like Ariba, not an aggregator like Moglix, and we do not sell a service to integrate third-party vendor catalogs into someone else's Ariba. Said plainly: "We are not an aggregator today."

How we recovered

We reframed the tail-spend problem as having three solutions: (1) aggregator mode, (2) cataloging/rate contracts, and (3) autonomous sourcing. The first two they already understood (and Ariba already supports L2 punch-outs out of the box). We positioned #3 as the genuinely new, differentiated wedge — and pivoted the demo there.

Why the honesty paid off

By refusing to oversell a catalog-integration service we don't offer, we earned credibility with a sharp, skeptical room ("they know who's throwing, who can deliver"). The catalog team's narrow ask was a dead-end for us; conceding it cleanly is exactly what opened the door to the autonomous-sourcing and vendor-onboarding conversations that actually fit.

04

What we put on the screen

Everything was shown on a live Adani-flavoured tenant, not slideware — including a real sourcing event with vendor-side bidding. The capabilities that landed:

Autonomous sourcing, end-to-end

From a vague intake ("I want to buy glass bottles / steel-cage bearings"), the AI asked category-aware clarifying questions, auto-built the PR and a complete RFQ (SOW, payment terms, Q&A), selected vendors, launched a live event, captured bids, and even pre-filled / bid on a vendor's behalf.

AI technical + commercial evaluation

AI-generated vendor profiles, project-specific strengths/weaknesses/risks, scored justifications, and zip-attachment analysis. Commercial side normalizes disparate pricing formats into a side-by-side view, surfacing hidden discounts and structural differences.

Autonomous negotiation

A structured multi-round agent (R1 discovery → benchmark pressure → target push → "baffle" round) negotiating with many vendors in parallel, with a transparent should-cost model and cited benchmark sources. Demoed on the McAfee security-software case. Fully autonomous or human-led.

Award optimization in natural language

Award scenarios ("relationship-based," cherry-pick best-in-class, multi-supplier split) described in free text and converted by AI into parameterized configurations — positioned against Ariba Bid Optimizer, which "nobody uses because it's too hard to configure."

Spend insights

Category dashboards with live indices: a steel index with an annual-contract-lock opportunity, ocean freight benchmarked off Freightos with a disruption monitor and carrier scorecards, ENA, and lubricants priced off crude / base-oil / bright-stock / SN-150 / SN-500 formulas.

Vendor management & enrichment

AI-enriched vendor 360 (business overview, delivery performance via direct GRN integration, price indices, web/news/compliance intelligence, sanction screening), with configurable re-evaluation triggers and OEM-vs-dealer tagging.

Atlas — the differentiator that moved the room

A conversational, voice-driven agent that answered live, pulled up a spend dashboard on command, and opened & pre-filled an intake form for janitorial / catering services at the Ahmedabad office (~₹1.9 lakh estimate). Crucially, it spoke Hindi and Gujarati on request — directly relevant because Adani's business users are largely Gujarati-speaking. The room's verdict: "ours is reactive; this is interactive." Atlas is what they texted each other about.

06

The live opportunity: vendor onboarding

This is where the meeting actually converged — and where the follow-up is already moving. After the visit, on the drive to the airport, Devesh called Gaurav with pointed integration questions; his team sent the NDA the next morning.

The pain (in their words)
  • Onboarding is slow, fragmented and multi-owner — no single entity owns the vendor; spread across SAP, Ariba and MDG.
  • A deliberately stricter process (to de-risk bad vendors) has made it very lengthy.
  • Blocking / blacklisting is still done manually over email.
  • Third-party due diligence (their "TBDT" process) is commercially costly.
  • No structured OEM-vs-dealer tagging in MDG (e.g., SKF, Schaeffler, NSK), so AI can't make policy-aligned recommendations.
What they want
  • AI-driven, much faster onboarding (CPO referenced wanting a major reduction from the current state; we positioned ≥70% improvement).
  • Automated due diligence — adverse media, financial, legal/business risk, sanctions — via integration with external risk sources, replacing the costly TBDT and the email-based blacklist.
  • Sit on top of Ariba + MDG + SAP (the CPO was explicit: "your solution should be on top of all three").
  • Structured vendor tagging and master-data rationalization.
The integration questions Devesh asked (post-meeting call)

How does Aerchain's vendor-onboarding flow intersect with Ariba (the current front end) via API? How does that tie into the SAP vendor master and MDG? Can we start as the onboarding front end on Ariba first, with a future state of going SAP-direct if/when Ariba is replaced? These are the questions the integration architecture document must answer.

08

Risks & watch-outs

Expectation management

We are not the catalog/aggregator they originally asked IndiaMart for. The deck must explicitly map our capabilities to their 5-point brief and be honest about what we do not do (third-party catalog integration into their Ariba), so we don't get re-filed under "wrong fit" by the catalog team.

Claims to validate before they're in writing

Several things were positioned as available "today" that are really roadmap or stretch: automated vendor calling ("3–4 weeks"), Atlas GA ("next month"), should-cost for a full 5.5 km conveyor / project BOQ ("we believe we can"), and contract authoring / redlining (a stated gap). Confirm internally before committing in the deck or a pilot SOW.

Scale & complexity

~500 locations, 14+ SAP instances, acknowledged "big chaos" in master data, and a protective architect. Implementation risk is real; Devesh and Chintan both stressed how hard their landscape is. Lead with a contained onboarding pilot, not a big-bang promise.

Incumbents & internal builds

Deep Ariba investment plus a habit of building internally ("we built that vendor-assessment feature 6 months ago"). They will resist ripping out Ariba and will benchmark us against in-house work. Differentiate on autonomous AI + Atlas, and on speed-to-value for onboarding.
